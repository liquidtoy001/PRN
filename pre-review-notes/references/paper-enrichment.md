# Paper enrichment

Apply the confirmed `preferences.note_configuration.paper_enrichment` choice before drafting.
This setting is independent of note purpose and does not relax source coverage requirements.

## Three levels

- `off`: use the supplied course materials. Preserve references already present in them,
  but do not initiate additional literature research or append new recommendations.
- `reading-list` (recommended at intake): add a short chapter-end reading list. For each
  paper give a verified citation and direct DOI/publisher/repository link, why it matters,
  the question it answers, and recommended sections or figures actually inspected. Prefer
  the original paper and a small number of relevant variants or applications. Do not fill
  a quota with loosely related papers. Keep the main teaching body course-grounded.
- `integrated`: also weave verified paper explanations, equations, informative figures and
  application evidence into the appropriate concept sections. Label supplementary content
  and distinguish it from what the course explicitly teaches. Retain the reading list.

## Retrieval and evidence

Actually retrieve and inspect sources using available search, publisher or repository tools,
or supplied local papers. Verify title, authors, year and DOI or stable paper identifier.
Resolve preprint versus published versions; name which version was read. Establish the
original architecture paper from evidence, not a familiar survey or the latest variant.
Do not invent bibliographic metadata, citations, figure numbers or experimental results.

Abstract-only or inaccessible papers may be listed as abstract/metadata verified with that
limitation disclosed. Do not recommend unseen sections or use unseen figures, equations,
details or results. If retrieval is unavailable, complete the supported course content and
report the enrichment limitation; do not fabricate a reading list from memory. Do not bypass
access restrictions. No external upload of private course materials is needed to search titles.

Add papers to the source inventory/citation ledger with stable IDs, preserving all existing
course IDs. Store downloaded papers locally under the same privacy boundary as course slides.
For web-only sources, retain verified metadata, stable URL, access level and exact section
locators in the ledger. Distinguish paper PDF page numbers from printed page numbering.

## Model-specific depth

When a chapter describes a network, or the learner supplies a model-review rubric, map the
applicable items to coverage K IDs: original paper; structure and main features; diagram;
variants; new contribution/principles; advantages; limitations; key equations, metrics and
training-data structure; application and a separate successful application paper. Do not
force this structure onto unrelated foundational chapters. A supplied rubric determines
coverage; unresolved items must be disclosed rather than treated as completed.

Explain advantages as claims supported under stated datasets, baselines and metrics, and
separate authors' claims from the evidence and your interpretation. Application success
does not establish universal superiority. Define equation symbols and assumptions; preserve
experimental conditions and distinguish conceptual improvements from benchmark performance.

## Figures and wording

Follow `source-visuals.md` for extraction, cropping, redraws and final rendered checks. Inspect
the original figure and caption before use; cite the paper and figure number plus page.
Label crops, adaptations, redraws and added annotations. Explain how the image supports the
current concept. Do not reconstruct exact scientific results with generative imagery.

Prefer accurate paraphrases; clearly mark brief quotations. Attribution does not itself
grant image-reuse permission. For publicly shared outputs, check the applicable figure
licence or permission and preserve attribution; if reuse is not supported, link to the figure
or provide an appropriately sourced original explanation. Redrawing still requires citation
and is not automatically a way around permission requirements.

Record retrieved sources and access limitations in review.md, map incorporated visual V IDs
and knowledge K IDs in coverage.md, and account for extra source volume and generation usage
through the existing capacity and usage workflow. Reading-list-only items need not inflate
the chapter's taught-content coverage count.
