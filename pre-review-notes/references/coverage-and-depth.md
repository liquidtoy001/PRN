# Coverage and teaching depth

## Selected scope and page accounting

Inventory metadata or text samples do not constitute reading a slide deck. Inspect every
selected physical page, including figures, footnotes and exercise prompts. Record physical
and printed slide numbers separately when overlays cause them to diverge. If extraction
is truncated, continue in bounded chunks; inspect image-heavy pages visually. Mark unreadable
pages pending rather than assuming they contain no knowledge.

Use `assets/coverage-template.md` to create a local `coverage.md` sidecar. Each source has a
page ledger covering the entire selected range without unexplained gaps. Classify ranges as
content, animation/duplicate, administrative, or unreadable. Animation groups must list the
new information introduced by intermediate frames; retain that sequence in prose or a diagram.
Do not automatically divide page counts by a presumed animation factor. A figure can teach
content even if it has no extractable text. Read administrative pages for assessment guidance
before excluding logistics from the teaching body; distinguish historical from current facts.

For each distinct definition, equation, condition, notation convention, algorithm step,
figure conclusion, example, exercise, limitation and substantive aside, assign a stable K ID.
Use one row per independently checkable idea. Several ideas from the same page need several
rows; genuine repetition can reference the same K ID. Record source ID, exact page locator,
the idea, destination section, disposition and rationale. Dispositions:

- explained: the note actually teaches the point at appropriate depth;
- merged: duplicate presentation, with the canonical K ID and destination recorded;
- excluded: a reason such as non-teaching logistics or an explicitly narrowed user scope;
- pending: not read, unresolved, deferred, or not yet explained.

Do not exclude a minor teaching point merely because it seems unlikely to be assessed.
Optional topics retain their label and receive proportionate explanation unless the learner
excluded them. A source-by-source heading list is not a knowledge-point ledger.

## Length as a diagnostic

Count raw selected pages and effective content units separately. An effective unit is a
substantive slide or a verified animation group with its intermediate changes preserved.
State the grouping decisions; never report an opaque automatic estimate as verified.

For ordinary teaching notes from roughly 100 content-bearing slides, fewer than 10 substantive
A4 note pages should trigger an over-compression review. Use approximately one note page per
ten effective content units as a warning baseline, not a ceiling or universal minimum.
Dense mathematics may require substantially more. Many true duplicate frames or sparse
visual slides may justify fewer; record that reasoning in `review.md` after checking coverage.
Exclude covers, contents, audit appendices and blank pages from note-body counts. Judge length
at readable typography. Do not inflate fonts, margins, repeated prose or page breaks to pass.
Explicit cheat sheets and exam-cram triage use their agreed scope and disclosed omissions.

## Completion gate

Apply `source-visuals.md` to informative figures and tables. Map their visual IDs to the
knowledge points they teach and record reuse/adaptation/omission decisions. Check both the
visual information and its explanation; a paragraph mentioning a figure is not automatically
equivalent to preserving it. Pending essential visuals prevent complete coverage claims.

Compare the final note with original pages, not only with the ledger (the ledger can miss
ideas). Check that conditions, fine-print caveats, examples and figure conclusions survive.
Resolve all pending substantive points before claiming complete coverage of the selected scope.
For partial batches, name the completed page ranges and pending K IDs. Keep coverage and
reasoning evidence in sidecars; the student-facing PDF needs only a short boundary and useful
source locators, not pages of operational audit text.

The ledger is an agent-authored audit trail, not proof of exhaustive semantic understanding.
Neither a high coverage percentage nor enough pages certifies correctness. Review reasoning
independently and record source discrepancies. Missing official answers limits calibration
but does not justify omitting explanations that can be derived from the course material.
